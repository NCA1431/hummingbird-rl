Sandbox
Four modes
  • Recorded: the test episodes measured in Python, replayed frame by frame. Same seeds, same simulator: each one is one of the episodes behind the results.
  • Live: a new random situation at each "New flight", drawn like the training and test ones (conditions menu), flown in your browser by the controller chosen. Changing the controller keeps the situation, to compare them on the same one.
  • Sandbox: you build the scene and set the conditions, and up to four controllers fly it together. Opened from a live flight, it starts from that flight.
  • Play: the birds fly without end, in real time, after a target you move: drag it, or an obstacle, while they fly (Shift + drag: up and down; F: the target follows the mouse). Any controller of any task can fly; a bird that falls starts again from the start point. "The birds see the target" off: they are no longer told where it is (it turns grey), something no controller met in training.
Every panel can be moved: drag it by an empty part; a double click puts it back.
What you see
  • Orange ball: the target zone, 3 cm in radius. Go-to-point, obstacles and the last navigation waypoint: the bird must stay inside for 0.25 s, slower than 10 cm/s ("holding" in the panel). Hover: it must stay within 3 cm on average over the last 2 s. The other navigation waypoints are 4 cm balls, only to be crossed. Faded: already reached. The dashed line gives the target's height.
  • Brown spheres: obstacles; touching one ends the flight.
  • Trail: the path flown, blue when slow, red at 1 m/s and more; grey after the end of the episode. With several birds (sandbox), each trail has its bird's colour.
  • Blue arrow above the bird: the wind (50 cm = a wind as strong as the bird's weight). The wind is the same everywhere in the arena, so with several birds one arrow, above the bird followed, says it for all.
  • Orange-red wing: a wing that has lost force (fully coloured at 40%).
  • Air (the "air" menu): a simulation of the air around the followed bird, in a 40 cm box that follows it (1 cm cells; another bird inside the box moves its air too). The wings push the air down with exactly the bird's simulated thrust, where each wing is in its stroke, and the wind blows through the box; the air is computed as an incompressible fluid with Jos Stam's "Stable Fluids" method. Best in slow motion (×1/16, Side camera). smoke: smoke released around the bird and by its wings, carried by the air. laser sheet: tracer particles in a thin slice facing you, as in a lab where fog is lit by a laser sheet; each leaves a short streak along its motion, brighter when faster. vortex tubes: the surfaces wrapping the air's vortices (where it spins more than it is sheared), coloured by speed, as simulations of flapping flight show their wakes. speed, pressure (blue below, red above the surrounding air), spin: the smoke coloured by the air it is in; their range is shown next to the menu. The air does not act back on the bird, and 1 cm cells are too coarse for the flow around the feathers: what you see is the jet, its large vortices, the wind bending it, the ground spreading it.
  • Grey ring on the ground: the start point. Green disc: the arena, 1.5 m in radius and 1.2 m high, with 10 cm squares.
  • Timeline: red tick = sudden wing failure; hatched band = after the end of the episode (live and sandbox, after a loss of control: shown, not counted).
Conditions (Live mode menu; the recorded test episodes are in normal conditions, or sudden failure)
  • normal: a light breeze (up to 5% of the bird's weight) with gusts of 2%, and small sensor and actuator noise. What every agent was tested in.
  • randomised: wind up to 30% of the weight, gusts up to 10%, a bird 25% lighter to 25% heavier, and in 70% of the flights one wing weakened by up to 40% from the start. The agents marked "randomised" were trained in these conditions.
  • sudden wing failure: normal conditions, then one wing loses 40% of its force, at a random moment between 1.5 and 3 s.
  • calm air: no wind and no noise at all.
Controllers: PID and LQR are classical controllers designed by hand; the others are neural networks trained by reinforcement learning, named algorithm · how it was trained · training run (e.g. "PPO · randomised · #1"). "Transfer from obstacles": it started from a network trained on the Obstacles task. "Fine-tuned": trained in normal conditions, then in randomised ones ("longer": twice as many extra steps). "Best": the version saved at its best validation score, not the last one. All of them see the same 48 numbers, measured with noise: altitude, speed, tilt and spin, their previous command, the next two targets and the five nearest obstacles. None is told the wind, the bird's mass or a wing's damage.
